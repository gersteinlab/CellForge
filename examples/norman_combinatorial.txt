Develop a predictive model for combinatorial CRISPR activation (CRISPRa) in K562 cells,
using the dataset from Norman et al. (2019, Science).

Task definition:
- Input: the baseline gene expression profile of an unperturbed K562 cell, together
  with the identity of one or two genes targeted for transcriptional activation.
- Output: the predicted log-normalised gene expression profile after perturbation,
  across all measured genes.

Evaluation scenarios:
1. Unseen single perturbations — target genes absent from training entirely.
2. Unseen gene pairs — combinations held out from training where each constituent gene
   may have been observed individually. This is the primary scenario.
3. Unseen cell contexts — baseline profiles not observed during training.

Metrics, reported both genome-wide and restricted to differentially expressed genes:
- MSE, PCC, R2.

Differentially expressed genes are defined per perturbation by a Wilcoxon rank-sum test
against control cells with Benjamini-Hochberg correction, keeping genes with adjusted
p < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| > 0.5.

The scientific crux:
The combined effect of activating genes A and B together is frequently NOT the sum of
activating each alone. Synergy and antagonism are common and are the interesting part
of this dataset. A model that assumes additivity in latent space will score acceptably
on average and fail precisely on the perturbations that matter. The design must be able
to represent non-additive interaction between the two activated genes explicitly, and
the evaluation should report performance on strongly non-additive pairs separately from
the overall average.

Average metrics per perturbation rather than per cell.
