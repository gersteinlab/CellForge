Develop a predictive model that jointly estimates transcriptome and surface-protein
response to CRISPR perturbation, using the ECCITE-seq dataset from Papalexi et al.
(2021, Nature Genetics).

Task definition:
- Input: the baseline profile of an unperturbed cell — both RNA and surface protein —
  together with the identity of the CRISPR target gene.
- Output: the predicted post-perturbation profile in BOTH modalities: log-normalised
  expression across all measured genes, and abundance across the ~200 measured surface
  proteins.

Evaluation scenarios:
1. Unseen perturbations — target genes absent from training.
2. Unseen cell contexts — baseline profiles not observed during training.

Metrics, reported separately for the RNA readout and the protein readout, and for each
both across all features and restricted to differentially expressed features:
- MSE, PCC, R2
- Recall of differentially expressed features. Protein recall is the primary metric and
  the hardest part of this task.

Differentially expressed features are defined per perturbation by a Wilcoxon rank-sum
test against control cells with Benjamini-Hochberg correction, keeping features with
adjusted p < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| > 0.5.

Task-specific considerations:
- The two modalities have different noise models and very different dynamic ranges. RNA
  counts are sparse and over-dispersed; protein counts from antibody-derived tags are
  denser but carry substantial background. A single shared output head forces a bad
  compromise between the two.
- Protein abundance is not a deterministic function of its own transcript. Translation,
  degradation, and trafficking all intervene. The model should be able to learn the
  relationship between the modalities rather than assume it.
- Known gene-gene interaction structure may be used to propagate perturbation effects,
  if the design can justify the choice of network.

Average metrics per perturbation rather than per cell.
