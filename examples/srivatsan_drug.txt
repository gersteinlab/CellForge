Develop a predictive model for small-molecule drug response at single-cell resolution,
using the sci-Plex 3 dataset from Srivatsan et al. (2020, Science).

Task definition:
- Input: the baseline gene expression profile of an untreated cell, the identity of a
  compound, and the applied dose.
- Output: the predicted expression response after treatment.

Evaluation scenarios:
1. Unseen compounds — molecules absent from training.
2. Unseen doses — dose levels for a compound seen at other concentrations.
3. Unseen cell contexts — baseline profiles not observed during training.

Metrics, reported both genome-wide and restricted to differentially expressed genes:
- MSE, PCC, R2.

Differentially expressed genes are defined per compound-dose condition by a Wilcoxon
rank-sum test against vehicle controls with Benjamini-Hochberg correction, keeping
genes with adjusted p < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| > 0.5.

Task-specific considerations:
- Dose is continuous and its effect is typically non-linear and saturating. Treating
  dose as a categorical label discards that structure and cannot interpolate to an
  untested concentration.
- Drug response is heterogeneous across cells within the same well. A point estimate of
  the mean response hides that heterogeneity; a model that outputs a conditional
  distribution over responses, rather than a single profile, is preferred, and the
  evaluation should assess distributional fit and not only the mean.
- Chemical structure information may be used to generalise across compounds, if the
  design can justify it.

Average metrics per compound-dose condition rather than per cell.
