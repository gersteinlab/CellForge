Develop a predictive model for chromatin accessibility response to CRISPR perturbation
in K562 cells, using the single-cell ATAC-seq dataset from Liscovitch-Brauer et al.
(2021, Nature Biotechnology).

Task definition:
- Input: the baseline chromatin accessibility profile of an unperturbed K562 cell
  across approximately 200,000 peaks, together with the identity of the CRISPR target.
- Output: the predicted post-perturbation accessibility profile across all peaks.

Evaluation scenarios:
1. Unseen perturbations — CRISPR targets absent from training.
2. Unseen cell contexts — baseline accessibility profiles not observed during training.

Metrics:
- ROC-AUC and PR-AUC over peak accessibility. These are the primary metrics.
- Recall of peaks whose accessibility changes significantly under the perturbation.
- Report PR-AUC prominently: with this degree of class imbalance ROC-AUC alone looks
  flattering and hides poor performance on the peaks that actually move.

Task-specific considerations:
- The feature space is roughly an order of magnitude wider than the transcriptomic
  tasks and is extremely sparse. Per-cell coverage is low, so a single observed cell
  gives a very noisy view of its own accessibility landscape.
- Accessibility is near-binary rather than continuous. Regression losses designed for
  log-normalised expression are a poor fit; the loss should reflect the actual data
  type.
- A model that collapses toward the marginal accessibility profile — predicting the
  population average regardless of perturbation — will score deceptively well on
  aggregate metrics. The evaluation must be able to detect that failure mode, for
  example by reporting performance restricted to differentially accessible peaks.
- Peaks are not independent; accessibility is spatially correlated along the genome and
  organised into domains. The design may exploit that structure.

Average metrics per perturbation rather than per cell.
